# Sources — tag, don't quote

These are one-paragraph, plain-language summaries of each organization's
general training philosophy, written to let the agent tag a complex's
`sources` field honestly. They are NOT citations of specific studies —
never attribute a specific statistic or finding to any of these unless
you have actually retrieved and verified the real source. See AGENTS.md
"Sourcing rule" for the hard constraint.

- **NSCA** (National Strength & Conditioning Association) — general
  periodization, max-effort/repeat-effort/dynamic-effort methodology,
  foundational strength and power frameworks.
- **UKSCA** (UK Strength & Conditioning Association) — athlete
  monitoring and readiness practices, long-term athletic development.
- **ASCA** (Australian S&C Association) — team-sport conditioning and
  return-to-play frameworks.
- **EXOS** (formerly Athletes' Performance) — movement-prep systems,
  multi-planar warm-ups, integrated speed/strength programming.
- **ALTIS** (track & field) — sprint mechanics, deceleration and
  acceleration biomechanics, force-vector-driven exercise selection.
- **Olympic weightlifting centers** — explosive triple-extension,
  catch positions, rate of force development.
- **NFL Combine culture** — standardized testing metrics (broad jump,
  40-yard dash), explosive-power benchmarking.
- **Soccer science (EPL/La Liga sports science literature)** — repeated
  sprint ability, small-sided conditioning, non-contact injury (hamstring,
  ACL) prevention research.
- **FMS** (Functional Movement Systems) — movement screening, identifying
  asymmetries and mobility restrictions before loading.
- **Run Lab / running mechanics research** — gait analysis, ground
  contact time, elastic/tendon stiffness training.

When tagging a complex, pick the organization(s) whose general approach the
complex reflects. Example: a complex built around a controlled deceleration
pattern might tag `["ALTIS", "FMS"]`. If nothing clearly fits, leave
`sources` empty — an empty tag is honest; a guessed one is not.
